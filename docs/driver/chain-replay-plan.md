# Chain replay: decision and plan (2026-09-19, desk study, no card run)

Paths are under `/Volumes/512SSD/EGPU_MAC_Nvidia/` unless absolute. `exec.c` = `cuda-shim/libtinynv/src/exec.c`, `ggml-cuda.cu` = `llama.cpp/ggml/src/ggml-cuda/ggml-cuda.cu`.

## 1. Decision

**Do not build Plan 2 as written ("record/replay at the libtinynv level, nothing from llama.cpp"); it cannot work as a design, and the #27330 reason for deferring it was borrowed from a mechanism we do not run.** Without a capture boundary the driver cannot know a token's shape until it has seen all ~2,000 launches, so it can either dispatch speculatively (a stale KV-write position is a corrupted cache, not a refusal) or wait for the whole token before dispatching (strictly worse than today's pipelined chains). **Build first the four cheap card reads in §7 — they cost no code, they measure exactly the two terms replay could remove (chain seams and first-chain build latency), and they bound replay's payoff at dense +2.5–3.5%, MoE +5–7% realistic.** If those reads say the terms are real, build replay in its only viable form: the CUDA-graph API subset in `libtinycudart` over a one-chain-per-token template in `libtinynv`, with ggml's own capture policy (already written, `ggml-cuda.cu:4420-4476`) deciding when a token is replayable.

## 2. What a token costs today, and what replay could remove

**Measured today** (`docs/handoff-2026-09-19.md:226-270`, interleaved tg128, three knobs on): dense 27B 70.7 tok/s = **14.1 ms/token** vs native 75.3 = 13.3 ms (gap **0.86 ms, 6%**); MoE 35B-A3B ~166 = **6.0 ms** vs native 245 = 4.1 ms (gap **1.9 ms, 32%**). Launches per token: dense **1,992**, MoE **1,634** (`docs/driver/libtinynv-design.md:435-436`), i.e. ~16 and ~13 chains of 128 (`exec.h:40`, `:119`).

**Per launch on the card.** The depth sweep gives `gap(d) = 1.0 + 22/d` µs (`exec.c:1033`), and a chain handover is "about fifteen microseconds" (`exec.h:116`); the 1.28 µs floor at depth 128 (`libtinynv-design.md:284`) is therefore ~1.0 µs of within-chain dispatch — already at the vendor's ~1 µs — plus a **15–22 µs seam per chain** amortised. Each seam is real engine idleness: the next chain's batch opens with an acquire on the previous tail value (`exec.c:1236-1239`), a cache invalidate (`:1241`), and a `SEND_PCAS` of the head (`:1248`), and the release it waits for lands in host memory across the link.

**Per launch on the host:** ~1.05 µs (`exec.c:73-78`), against 7.1 µs (dense) and 3.7 µs (MoE) of card time per launch, so the host runs ahead all token and is on the critical path only at the boundary.

**The boundary, after today's change.** The copy-engine handoff (~0.35 ms/token, `libtinynv-design.md:513-550`) is gone: 1561/1561 dense flushes rode in the compute batch (`:1040-1046`). What remains: llama.cpp's ~150 µs of sampling and graph rebuild (not ours), then the engine gets nothing until 128 launches are built (**~130–160 µs**), one push+submit, and ~45–60 µs of pickup (`libtinynv-design.md:465`). The ~2.5 KB of delta patches cost ~10 µs (`:1043`).

**What "keep the chain resident, patch only the changes" removes, precisely.** The delta rewind already makes the descriptor region a resident template: launch k of every token lands where launch k of the last one did (`exec.c:1586-1604`), only ~23 patches per 128-launch flush go out (`logs/serve-20260919-092014.log:1935`: 11,145,874 patches / 481,210 flushes), and the tail alone releases (`exec.c:981-983`). So replay does **not** remove descriptor traffic — that is done. It does **not** remove the card fetching and dispatching each descriptor (~1.0 µs × 2,000, identical to native's graph-launched gap as far as anything measured here says), nor kernel time, nor llama.cpp's 150 µs, nor the logits download or the input uploads. What a one-chain-per-token replay removes:

| term | dense | MoE |
|---|---|---|
| 15 (resp. 12) seams × 15–22 µs | 0.22–0.33 ms | 0.18–0.26 ms |
| first-chain build before the engine restarts | 0.13–0.16 ms | 0.13–0.16 ms |
| host rebuild + diff of ~200 KB per flush | off the critical path; matters only under many slots or the MTP draft loop, which stood still 252,833 times in 481,210 flushes (`serve…log:1936`) |
| **realistic total** | **0.35–0.5 ms = +2.5–3.5%** | **0.31–0.42 ms = +5–7%** |
| **upper bound** (submit and pickup also shrink) | ~+4% | ~+9% |

**The MoE gap is mostly elsewhere.** Native's own graph benefit is ~10–15% of decode (#27330 comment, giovaneHR; PR #6766's +14%), so native MoE *without* graphs is ~215–220 tok/s; we are 25% below that with a mechanism that already has the GPU-side property graphs give. Replay's ceiling explains ≤0.5 ms of the 1.9 ms. The rest is inside kernel-executing time or in per-descriptor choices the oracle also makes — every QMD carries five cache invalidates and `CWD_MEMBAR_TYPE=L1_SYSMEMBAR` (`qmd.c:170-183`, `tinygrad/tinygrad/runtime/ops_nv.py:291`), a system-scope barrier at every kernel end over Thunderbolt. **The one measurement that decides replay's MoE ceiling:** `gpuPercentBusy`/`memoryPercentBusy` (`tinynv.c:768-769`) sampled through a MoE tg256 with the three knobs, as §4f did at 130 tok/s (`libtinynv-design.md:376-437`). Dry time ≥1.2 ms/token → seams and boundary still hold it and replay is a lever; ≤0.6 ms → the gap is in kernel-executing time and replay cannot reach it.

**One thing the logs cannot tell me.** With ggml's graph reuse, kernel parameters must be token-invariant (ggml re-captures on any tensor-struct or pointer change, `ggml-cuda.cu:2595-2635`; native would be slow if that happened per token). Yet ~22 small patches per flush beyond the tail payload survive the rewind, and no driver-internal launch runs per token by default (`TINYNV_DOWNLOAD_VIA_COMPUTE` is opt-in, `tinynv.c:283-290`; memset is a staged copy, `:656-660`). Their identity — which launch index, which dwords — is exactly what `TINYNV_DELTA_VERBOSE` prints for the first three flushes (`exec.c:1092-1094`, `:1131-1133`) and nobody has run it since the rewind landed. It is the field classifier's ground truth and a one-line read on the next card session; if those dwords are kernel parameters, a capture template cannot be frozen and the design in §5 must patch them.

## 3. What CUDA graphs do for native, and which half we already have

In the vendored ggml (`llama.cpp` at b906d25): graphs are on by default (`llama.cpp/ggml/CMakeLists.txt:209`), keyed by the cgraph's first node (`ggml-cuda.cu:2591-2593`, cache with 10 s eviction `common.cuh:1474-1495`), disabled only below Volta or by `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS` (`:4404-4418`, `common.cuh:1297-1300`). Compatibility excludes only a `MUL_MAT_ID` that needs a stream sync (`:2558-2589`, `:1881-1906`) — a quantized MoE at decode batch takes mmvq and stays graph-eligible, so **the Windows 245 tok/s MoE reference ran with graphs**. Warm-up needs two consecutive calls with identical node properties (`:4440-4460`); then every token is one `cudaGraphLaunch` (`:4395`) with **no per-node patching at all**: the cpy-node KV-destination patching of PR #6766 is gone (no `cudaGraphKernelNodeSetParams` anywhere in `ggml-cuda.cu`; `nodes`/`num_nodes` in `common.cuh:1281-1282` are vestigial), and a property change re-captures (`:4471`, `:4374`) then `cudaGraphExecUpdate` or re-instantiate (`:2637-2662`). Two kernels change behaviour under capture (`argsort.cu:76-84`, `mean.cu:28-45`).

**The advantage has two halves.** CPU side: 2,000 `cudaLaunchKernel` calls at NVIDIA's ~2–4 µs each is 4–8 ms of CPU per token — *more than the MoE's 4.1 ms GPU time* — which is why graphs matter natively for MoE. We pay this differently: 1.05 µs per launch (`exec.c:73-78`), below the card's own per-launch time, so our host is never the bottleneck and this half is already banked. GPU side: descriptors pre-built and resident, each scheduling the next, one submission per token. Our chain has the first two properties (`exec.c:1668-1670`, `qmd.c:261-279`, one launch method per 128 at `:1248`); it lacks only "one submission per token", which is the seam term in §2. The prior-art note's claim that replay "removes per-token CPU rebuild AND seam drains" (`docs/research/launch-overhead-prior-art.md:33-34`) is still true, but §4f has since shown both terms are a few hundred microseconds, not the milliseconds the note assumed.

## 4. #27330: what it is, which layer, and whether we share it

**Facts** (issue and its 9 comments, fetched today; open, unbisected, no maintainer comment, no fix): under sustained `llama-server` load — long contexts, multiple slots, prompt caching/checkpoints, MTP or dflash speculation — the GPU stops servicing the channel; the kernel driver's RC watchdog fires **Xid 8** after 7 s and llama-server dies with "the launch timed out and was terminated" inside `cudaStreamSynchronize`. Stochastic, 4 min to hours in. Reproduced on desktop and laptop 5090s, RTX PRO 4000 Blackwell, a 4090+5080 split, Windows (616.92) and Linux (595.84–595.91); the sibling #28404 (two 5060 Ti processes, Windows, deterministic CUDA error right after "CUDA Graph id … reused") shares the workaround. `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS=1` is a complete workaround at a 10–15% decode cost. One reporter notes 30–300 s *self-recovering* stalls persist with graphs off. NVIDIA's own 595.91.07 notes fix "Xid 32 errors and application crashes … with CUDA graphs that have a large number of chained kernel nodes" — the driver's graph executor is an active bug surface on this generation.

**Layer.** Xid 8 is a channel that stops being serviced, not a fault: a stale-pointer patch in ggml would fault (Xid 13/31/43 — cf. #21564, a Blackwell Xid 43 from a kernel bug), and there is no per-node patching left in ggml to go stale. The evidence points at the NVIDIA driver's graph execution path — how `cudaGraphExec` turns the DAG into channel work and dependency semaphores under memory pressure and re-instantiation churn. **Our driver runs none of that code.** A replayed token would be the same dependent-QMD chain that runs every token today, submitted through the same acquire/PCAS batch (`exec.c:1236-1272`); its failure modes are ours and all already have readings: a wrong link → fault or never-completes → 30 s timeout refusal (`exec.c:356-380`; recoverable at the next open, as the LoRA fault showed today, `handoff…:313-339`), a wrong tail payload → the wait's timeout refusal, a frozen parameter gone stale → a wrong answer under the same guard native relies on. **The "same mechanism could lock our card" concern is borrowed.** The one honest residual: if the native hang were in the hardware's handling of *very long* dependent-QMD chains (the release note says "chained kernel nodes"), a 2,000-link replay chain is a regime our 128-link chains have not entered. That is why the depth sweep in §7 doubles as the safety probe.

## 5. The design, if the reads justify it

**Form.** Implement in `libtinycudart` the subset ggml calls: `cudaStreamBeginCapture` (today `cudaErrorNotSupported`, `cudart_api.c:86`), `EndCapture`, `StreamIsCapturing`, `GraphInstantiate`, `GraphExecUpdate` (return success by rebinding; ggml already handles `cudaErrorGraphExecUpdateFailure` by re-instantiating, `ggml-cuda.cu:2647-2660`), `GraphLaunch`, `GraphDestroy`, `GraphExecDestroy`; build llama.cpp with `-DGGML_CUDA_GRAPHS=ON` (currently OFF, `docs/03-cuda-shim-plan.md:24`). **Nothing else is needed from llama.cpp**: ggml's uid/node_props guard is the shape-change detector (`:2595-2635`), its warm-up rule the record policy, its eviction the template lifetime. Streams are one exec (`tinynv.c:73`), so capture is a mode flag on `tinynv_exec_t`.

**Record.** In capture mode `tinynv_exec_run_inner` (`exec.c:1560-1692`) builds QMD+cbuf0 into a **template region** — a separate VRAM allocation, not `AR_DESC`, so the arena, rewind (`:1601-1604`, `arena.c:84-100`) and `last_delivered` (`:1197-1198`) are untouched — linking every launch into **one chain** with no per-descriptor release and a tail whose payload is a patch site. A captured `cudaMemcpyAsync`/memset becomes a launch of the shim's own copy kernel (`cudart_api.c:127`, `copy1d.cubin`) so the template stays one chain; anything else inside a capture is refused at `EndCapture`. Delivery once, by copy engine, at `EndCapture` (~3 MB). Fingerprint per launch (kernel descriptor, grid, block, smem, parameter bytes) kept for verify mode.

**Field classifier.** Under capture semantics there is none to write: everything is frozen except the tail release payload (2 dwords) — exactly tinygrad's answer. HCQGraph records once, touches the device timeline only at entry (`tinygrad-stable/tinygrad/runtime/graph/hcq.py:159`) and exit (`:217`), rebinds those two values per run (`:276`, `:290`), and zeroes its internal signals before each launch (`:295`); in the queue the release is attached to the last QMD's own release slot as patchable words (`tinygrad-stable/…/ops_nv.py:159-177`; new tree `ops_nv.py:143-146`, patches applied at submit `:149-152`), and a cross-queue wait deliberately breaks the chain (`:139-141`, our `exec.c:706-715`). This is `TINYNV_TAIL_RELEASE=1` plus one patch, which the delta path emits today (`exec.c:1215-1224`). If the §2 verbose read shows genuine per-token parameter dwords, they become additional patch sites at fixed template offsets, computed from the launch the shim would have built — that is the only case where a classifier exists, and it is then a table from the verbose dump, not an inference.

**Replay.** `cudaGraphLaunch` → flush the pending chain; `batch_begin` on the compute queue (drains the token's held input uploads ahead of the launch, `exec.c:718-726`); acquire on `q_last[0]`; patch the tail payload by inline upload before the launch method (the ordering the delta path already relies on); `SEND_PCAS(head)`; `submit_batch` with `upto` = one reserved value. Timeline accounting is unchanged: one value per replayed token, `in_chain_flush` window respected (`:819-823`), wraps and idles cover it because its tail is on the timeline.

**Shape-change failure mode and refusals.** ggml misses a change → frozen parameters compute on stale data → wrong text, no fault (native's exposure too). Our addition native cannot have: `TINYNV_REPLAY_VERIFY=N` runs every N-th token unreplayed and diffs the built descriptors against the template with `tinynv_delta_runs` (`exec.h:368-369`); any run outside the tail payload refuses with launch index and dword offset. A capture containing a non-launch refuses at `EndCapture` naming the op.

## 6. Risk class and validation

**Wrong replay looks like:** stale offset/pointer into live memory → wrong token (verify mode catches it; greedy byte-compare catches it); pointer into freed memory → MMU fault, reported with the address (`exec.c:361-364`); bad link or payload → hang at the next wait → 30 s timeout refusal, recovery at the next open; a wedged GSP → replug, the standing hazard of any hang, not specific to replay.

**Offline** (the null device prints the launch stream but never reaches `exec.c`, `tinynv.c:965-970`, `exec.c:834-837`): the capture state machine and `EndCapture` refusal against `llama-simple-null` streams for both models with graphs ON (proves a decode token captures only launches or copy kernels); a `test_delta`-style unit test that records a synthetic 2,000-link template and checks every link and the single release with `tinynv_qmd_link_check` (`qmd.c:224-259`); the verify-mode diff against recorded templates with planted stale dwords.

**On the card, in order:** (1) `TINYNV_DELTA_VERBOSE=1` tg16 both models — read the ~22 fields; (2) `TINYNV_SHORT_FIRST_CHAIN=8` with the three knobs — measures the boundary-build term now that the copy handoff it lost to is gone (`libtinynv-design.md:537-541`); expect +1% dense, +2% MoE, refusal reading: no change → the term is not there; (3) chain depth 256/512/1024 (needs `exec.h:40` raised; `:56` holds) with the knobs — measures the seam term and enters the long-chain regime; op-verify 450/450 at each depth, then a 20-minute 8-slot soak at the deepest; (4) the MoE busy counters. Then, only if (2)+(3)+(4) put ≥3% on the table: (5) `test_hw_chain`-style 2,000-link template replayed 10,000 times, tail payload checked each time; (6) op-verify with graphs ON; (7) 96-token greedy byte-compare, both models, against today's references; (8) interleaved tg128; (9) **soak ≥60 min, 8 slots, MTP on, greedy probes byte-identical**, because #27330's regime was hours of multi-slot long-context serving with constant re-capture — today's 5-minute one-slot soak (`handoff…:273-289`) would not have seen it.

## 7. Ordering against the knob flip

Flip the three knobs first — the pre-flip list is complete (`handoff…:291-339`) and every step above should be measured on the new defaults, not against them. Then one card session for reads (1)–(4); they are the go/no-go for replay and, if no-go, they name the next lever: the busy counters split the MoE gap between "dry engine" (short first chain, depth) and "kernel-executing" (per-QMD choices — `L1_SYSMEMBAR` on non-releasing descriptors, the five invalidates — each an A/B with op-verify and a byte-compare, none needing new mechanism).

## The four reads, done 2026-09-19 12:15-12:38 (Antonio's go), and the verdict

All on the new defaults, driver `4714f86`+diagnostics (`745fcd4`), interleaved in the same minutes, host load 4.4-7.3
(the MoE numbers of this hour sit ~15% under the morning's at load 2.5-4 on both paths; ratios, not levels, are what
the reads compare). Logs `logs/gate-steps/step33-*` to `step39-*`.

1. **The fields (`TINYNV_DELTA_VERBOSE`, 64-token decodes, both models).** For the first ~4 decode tokens after the
   prompt every flush changes 40-77 KB: whole QMDs (dwords 4-19, 32-59, 96-127) and driver-parameter bytes of cbuf0 in
   every launch - ggml's allocator still moving buffers, so each token's launch sequence differs from the last and every
   later launch shifts. From then on **every full-depth flush rounds to 0 KB: only the tail release payload differs
   token to token** (the 2.0-2.7 KB per-flush averages of the day are those early tokens amortised). There is no
   per-token kernel parameter in the descriptors; positions and KV lengths live in tensor data. A replay template would
   patch the release alone - tinygrad's answer - and would have to re-record through the settling tokens, as ggml
   re-captures.
2. **Short first chain (`TINYNV_SHORT_FIRST_CHAIN=8`)**: MoE 140.0 / 140.1 vs 140.7 / 141.0, dense 67.9 / 68.3 vs 68.5 /
   68.6. **No change** - the boundary-build term is not there.
3. **Chain depth (ceiling raised to 1024, default 128 kept, `TINYNV_CHAIN_DEPTH`)**: op-verify 450/450 at 256, 512 and
   1024. tg128, two rounds: MoE 128: 139.6/137.2, 256: 133.2/141.3, 512: 128.6/133.6, **1024: 119.0/120.1**; dense 128:
   68.3/69.2, 256: 66.3/67.1, 512: 62.8/63.3, **1024: 57.2/57.2**; downward, MoE 32: 123.7/119.4, 64: 135.0/128.7, 128:
   132.1/135.1; dense 32: 67.2/66.9, 64: 67.7/67.8, 128: 68.0/67.6. **Deeper is monotonically slower**: the engine
   cannot start a chain until the host has built all of it, so at 1024 it waits ~1 ms per chain where at 128 the next
   chain's build already hides the seam. The seam term is not on the critical path. The long-chain regime is safe.
4. **Busy counters through tg256 (publisher now on the flush path)**: MoE gpu busy 66%, mem busy 6%, 2862 MHz, 154 W
   mean / 223 peak, pstate 1, no throttling; dense busy 78%, mem 35%, 306 W mean / 459 peak. The card boosts fully.

**Verdict: do not build launch-chain replay.** Both terms it could recover are already absent or hidden (reads 2 and 3),
and the descriptor traffic it would remove was removed by the delta defaults. The MoE gap (~32% to native) sits inside
"busy" time - kernel execution plus what each descriptor makes the engine do at its tail (five invalidates and
`CWD_MEMBAR_TYPE=L1_SYSMEMBAR` on every QMD, `qmd.c:170-183`, a system-scope barrier over Thunderbolt per kernel) -
and the 34% dry fraction is the ~1 us inter-kernel dispatch gap native has too. **The next lever is the per-QMD tail:**
an A/B of the invalidates and the membar scope on non-releasing descriptors, each with op-verify at three depths and
the byte-compare, none needing new mechanism.
