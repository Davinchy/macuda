An RTX 3060 over Thunderbolt, against the M3 Max's own GPU

The same four models on the same Mac, twice. Once on a 12 GB RTX 3060 in a Razer Core X V2, driven by macuda's own userspace driver with no NVIDIA software on the machine. Once on the M3 Max through Apple Metal.

RTX 3060, macuda (sm_86, cubins built by nvcc in a container) M3 Max, Metal (llama.cpp and stable-diffusion.cpp Metal backends)

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Q4_K_M

4.36 GiB of weights, all of them on the GPU on both sides. llama-bench, 256-token prompt, 128 generated, three repetitions.

Qwen3.8-27B, unsloth UD Q4_K_M

15.32 GiB of weights and 65 layers. The M3 Max holds all of it. The 3060 holds 44 layers in its 12 GB and leaves 21 to the CPU, or 38 layers when its MTP draft head (1.27 GiB) shares the card. Speculative decoding paid on neither side here: the 3060 is bound by its CPU layers, and the Metal draft path ran far slower than plain decoding.

Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Q4_K_M

A mixture of experts: 20.5 GiB of weights, 256 experts per layer, 8 used per token, about 3B parameters active. The M3 Max holds all of it. The 3060 holds the attention and the experts of 13 of its 40 layers; the other 27 layers' experts run on the CPU (-ncmoe 27).

SDXL-Turbo, fp16

stable-diffusion.cpp, 4 steps, 512×512, cfg 1.0, seed 42. Wall time for one image, weights already in the page cache.

How it was measured

The card. ASUS RTX 3060 12 GB (GA106, device 0x2504) in a Razer Core X V2 on a Thunderbolt link at x4, 16 GT/s. The driver is libtinynv from this repository: VBIOS falcon boot, GSP-RM 570.144, QMD v3 descriptors, MMU v2 page tables. Every card step was run through tools/nv_shim_step.sh with QUIESCE=1, after a read-only preflight, and the card was booted cold each time: the driver unloads GSP-RM and tears its region down on the way out.

The Mac. Apple M3 Max with 48 GB of unified memory, powered from Apple's 140 W adapter, not from the enclosure. Metal builds of llama.cpp (at the same commit the card binaries link against) and stable-diffusion.cpp, with the Metal library embedded.

What is comparable. Prefill and decode are llama-bench's own tokens per second. The 7B is a like-for-like comparison: all weights on both GPUs. The 27B is not: the 3060 runs a third of it on the CPU, so its numbers describe a 12 GB card with a 15 GiB model, not the card's compute. The image times are from the same binary's stage timings; the 3060's include copying the weights over Thunderbolt, and the first Metal run, at 13.57 s, spent 6 s reading the file cold and is not the number shown.

Correctness. The 3060 passes the value-checked test-backend-ops suite 450/450, its SDXL-Turbo image is byte-identical to the first image the card ever produced (2026-09-17), and its 96-token greedy decode is kept as a reference text. The Metal greedy text diverges from the card's at character 156, which is the expected result of different kernels accumulating in a different order.
